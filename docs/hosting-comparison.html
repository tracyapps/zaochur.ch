How to read jurisdiction here (the plain-language version)

  • A · EU company, EU servers strongest posture
  • B · Non-US company, EU regions outside direct US reach
  • C · US company, EU regions available EU data centers, US parent

The US CLOUD Act lets US courts compel US-incorporated companies to hand over data they control — regardless of where the servers sit. A German or French or Singaporean company is outside that direct reach; requests must go through slower international channels. GDPR applies whenever data is processed in the EU, whoever the owner is. So: “EU region” on a US platform is real but partial protection; an EU (or non-US) company is defense-in-depth. Per our threat model, jurisdiction is one layer — minimal retention is the stronger one.

What “Zao scale” assumes

30–150 members · always-on (no sleeping apps — a community never sleeps) · Postgres ≤ 8 GB · file storage ≤ 100 GB · 3–10k digest emails/mo · light video: a few gatherings/week. Estimates are planning ranges, not quotes; € shown as ≈$ at ~1.08.

⚠ Two traps we verified: Supabase’s free tier pauses projects after ~1 week of inactivity (disqualifying for an always-on Commons — budget Pro), and Vercel’s Hobby plan is personal/non-commercial only (an organization like Zao belongs on Pro).
