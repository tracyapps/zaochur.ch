# Product Principles

> **Status:** Draft v0.1 — for community review
> **Derived from:** the [Non-Negotiables](02-non-negotiables.md). If a principle here ever seems clever and the non-negotiables seem inconvenient, trust the non-negotiables.

These are the tastes and instincts every screen, feature, and notification will be judged against. They are what make the Commons feel like *ours* instead of like every other app.

## 1. Calm by default

The Commons' promise: *open me, be with your people, close me, be at peace.*

- No unread counts chasing you. No red dots by default. No infinite scroll, anywhere, ever.
- "You're all caught up" is a real state, celebrated, and reachable.
- Quiet hours are the default, not a hidden toggle. Nights and Sundays belong to God and rest.
- Real-time pings are rare and opt-in. The default news of the community is a **gentle digest** — daily or weekly, your choice — that can be read in two minutes and never grows teeth.
- **Overwhelm is a design failure.** If a member says the app makes their chest tight, that report is treated like a crash.

## 2. The building is the map

We already named our Discord after rooms of the church. The Commons makes that metaphor real, gently:

- A calm, two-dimensional floor plan — **not** 3D, not gamified, not a metaverse. Blueprint-like, warm, slow to change, easy to learn once.
- Rooms are places with doors: the Front Door, the Sanctuary, the Living Room, the Library, the Rec Room, the Music Studio, the Art Gallery, the Neighborhood, plus the Harbors and Affinity Spaces. (Full map: [What We're Building](05-what-we-are-building.md).)
- **Presence without surveillance:** "A few folks are in the Living Room" — gentle, opt-in, never logged or historized (see the [Threat Model](03-safety-and-threat-model.md)). Knowing who's gathered is what makes a building feel alive rather than a channel list feel like homework.
- Avatars and chosen names, because a room with faces is a room with friends.
- You enter a room; you don't "join a channel." You sit near people; you don't "mention" them.

## 3. Async-first, presence-gentle

- Nobody must be online at a moment to matter. Everything meaningful has an unhurried window: events, games rounds, discussions, even watch parties (recorded when appropriate and consented).
- Cameras optional. Voice optional. Text always honored as a full way to participate.
- **Body doubling and quiet company are designed rooms** — being together without performing togetherness.

## 4. Restraint is a feature

Every feature we *don't* build buys the community speed, safety, and rest. We measure success by whether people leave the Commons feeling *more* alive (it's in the name), not by minutes-in-app.

## 5. From viewer to true participant

Our Join page promises that entering the Digital Commons moves you "from viewer to true participant in church life." The Commons is built to keep that promise: low-friction entry, a warm front door, a tour from a human being, and every important thing (worship, mutual aid, play, care) reachable within one gentle screen of arrival.

## 6. Reputation is narrative, not mathematical

Inherited from ZAO Bank: no leaderboards of worth, no scores for humans, no star ratings of people. We celebrate *participation and gifts* in words — appreciations, thank-yous, stories — never in rankings. Where games keep score (games may!), the score attaches to the game and the day, never to a person's standing in the community. See the games nuance in [What We're Building](05-what-we-are-building.md).

## 7. Abundance in the bones

The Good News shows us God's abundance against scarcity's hoarding. So: the Commons Bank runs on gifts and soft reciprocity, not strict ledgers of debt. The give-away shelf is gifts, not sales. Nothing expires maliciously; nobody "loses" standing by resting. A community member who rests for six months returns fully a member.

## 8. Plain language, warm voice

The Commons speaks like Zao speaks: warm, direct, human. Error messages say what happened and what to do next. No "Oops! Something went wrong." No jargon. If a sentence needs a technical dictionary, it's wrong.

## 9. Boring where it doesn't matter, beautiful where it does

Infrastructure should be forgettable; the rooms, rituals, and art should be lovely. We spend our small budget of novelty on the experience of being together, and nowhere else.

---

## What we will *not* build

This list is a covenant, not a backlog:

- **Engagement machinery:** infinite scroll, streak-guilt, "you were missed 😢", "87 people are active right now," algorithmic feeds, any "for you" ranking.
- **Dark patterns:** nagging re-permission prompts, confirm-shaming, hidden opt-outs, urgency timers.
- **People-rankings and reputation scores** of any kind (games' daily scores excepted — see the nuance above).
- **Surveillance:** read receipts on by default, typing indicators in sacred spaces, online-status history, "last seen," attention analytics.
- **3D/spatial worlds:** motion sickness, device cost, interface confusion — the opposite of ACCESS-1 through ACCESS-3. The floor plan is flat on purpose.
- **Real-time internet jamming** in the Music Studio: physics won't allow it. We'll do layer-recording instead, beautifully.
- **Ads, sponsored anything, data partnerships, "login with Big Tech," crypto/web3, AI chatbots posing as members or pastors.** (AI may assist *builders* behind the scenes, as a power tool — never as a participant in community life, and never trained on our members' words.)
- **Growth features.** We are not trying to grow. We are trying to be church, wherever we are.
