# Non-Negotiables

> **Status:** Draft v0.1 — for community review
> **What this is:** The constitution of the Zao Commons. These five commitments are why-driven, not feature-driven. Every roadmap item, technology choice, design decision, and partnership must be checked against them. When they conflict with a deadline or a convenience, **the deadline moves and the convenience gives way.**

These commitments inherit their ethics from the ZAO Bank safety design, which the community already discerned together:

> *Hospitality over borders. Safety without carceality. Memory without surveillance. Accountability with grace.*

Each commitment below has an ID (like `SAFETY-3`) so other documents can cite it precisely.

---

## 1. Safety

**Why:** We are a community of people targeted by the powers of this moment — our pastors have already had to leave the country for their family's safety. A tool that cannot protect the most vulnerable among us protects no one. We align with Jesus, who always aligns with the oppressed.

**Commitments:**

- **SAFETY-1 · Safety without carceality.** No automated bans, shadowbans, or punishments. When content is flagged, exposure is reduced immediately (hidden, not deleted); *humans* — trained, accountable, and rested — determine outcomes. ([Governance & Care](08-governance-and-care.md))
- **SAFETY-2 · Pseudonymity is a shelter.** No legal-name, phone-number, or government-ID requirements, ever. Pseudonyms and chosen names are first-class identities. For some members, a pseudonym is the difference between community and danger.
- **SAFETY-3 · Minimal target surface.** We collect and retain as little identifying data as possible (see the [Threat Model](03-safety-and-threat-model.md) retention schedule), because we cannot leak, subpoena, or seize what we never stored.
- **SAFETY-4 · A written plan for the worst day.** Before launch, we will have a rehearsed incident plan: server compromise, doxxing attempt, legal demand, or the loss of a key caretaker. It will name who acts, how members are warned, and how the community regathers.
- **SAFETY-5 · Visibility under duress.** Prayer requests, Harbor attendance, affinity group membership, and DMs carry visibility controls ("pastors only," "community only," "just a few people") because participation must never out anyone.

**How we hold ourselves accountable:** Caretaking incidents are logged (without exposing reporters) and reviewed each season with the community told what happened and what changed. A safety commitment that is never tested is a wish, not a shelter.

## 2. Privacy

**Why:** Our beliefs name the messages of scarcity and hoarding in capitalism — surveillance is that same logic applied to human beings. Our life together is not a product. The database should tell the truth quietly, and only to those it belongs to.

**Commitments:**

- **PRIVACY-1 · No ads. No tracking. No selling. Not now, not ever, not "to keep the lights on."** If the lights can only stay on by selling us, the lights go out and we regather by candlelight.
- **PRIVACY-2 · Structural, not a settings page.** Privacy cannot depend on an exhausted person finding option 47 of 90. Defaults protect; opting *in* to sharing is the act that requires intent.
- **PRIVACY-3 · Our data is ours.** Every member can export or delete their data at any time, in a plain format, without asking permission. The community can export the whole Commons, so it can never be held hostage by any tool or host — including our own.
- **PRIVACY-4 · Jurisdiction is a safety decision.** Data lives in jurisdictions whose laws protect rather than pursue our members. (Current proposal: EU/GDPR — see [Technology](07-technology.md) and [Decision Log](09-decision-log.md).)
- **PRIVACY-5 · No dark analytics.** We measure whether the space *serves* (can people find events? are digests read? is anyone overwhelmed?) — never who is *most engaged*, never individual attention, never anything a boss, platform, or bad actor would want a leaderboard of.
- **PRIVACY-6 · Third parties get the same covenant.** Every vendor we touch (email, hosting, media) is chosen and reviewed against these commitments. A chain of custody is only as strong as its weakest covenant.

**How we hold ourselves accountable:** A plain-language "what we store and why" page, written for humans, published with the app. An annual privacy review, in the open.

## 3. Accessibility

**Why:** Our own website promises a space "both sacred and accessible." Disability, chronic illness, and mental health are not edge cases in this community — they are the community. Jesus centered those the world pushed to the margins; so does our UI.

**Commitments:**

- **ACCESS-1 · WCAG 2.2 AA as the floor, not the ceiling.** Keyboard-complete, screen-reader-first-class, color-contrast-safe, motion-respecting. Accessibility bugs are launch blockers, not backlog items.
- **ACCESS-2 · Low-energy modes are core, not " lite."** A text-first, low-bandwidth, low-motion, low-scroll experience that works on an old phone and a tired brain. Reduced motion and plain views are defaults, not easter eggs.
- **ACCESS-3 · Calm is an accessibility feature.** For members with ADHD, anxiety, or overwhelm, notifications and unread counts *are* the barrier. Quiet defaults, batched digests, "you're caught up" states, and zero infinite scroll are accessibility work ([Product Principles](04-product-principles.md)).
- **ACCESS-4 · Many ways to be present.** Async-first: nobody must be online at a given moment to participate fully. Cameras optional, voice optional, text always honored. Body doubling and quiet companionship are designed rooms, not afterthoughts.
- **ACCESS-5 · Plain language everywhere.** No jargon walls. If a member needs to understand tech to be safe, we have failed. (These documents are held to the same standard — call it out when we miss.)
- **ACCESS-6 · Tested by the people it's for.** Disabled members are recruited and compensated (from the Commons Bank, if they wish) as accessibility reviewers, not as free labor.

**How we hold ourselves accountable:** Accessibility review before every release, by humans, with findings published. A standing "barriers" thread where anyone can name an obstacle and expect a response, not a defense.

## 4. Intentional Inclusion

**Why:** "Radically inclusive" is a pillar, and our Statement of Beliefs names it plainly: BIPOC people, LGBTQ folx, disabled people, and all harmed by oppression are made in the image of God. Inclusion that isn't intentional drifts to whoever the tool was built for by default — which is never us.

**Commitments:**

- **INCLUSION-1 · The door is wide.** No Discord account, no app store, no specific phone, no tech fluency, no trust in big tech required. A link and a welcome.
- **INCLUSION-2 · Center the margins in design.** For every feature we ask: who does this serve least, and what would change if they were the first person we designed it for? (This is "reading from the margins," applied to software.)
- **INCLUSION-3 · Affinity spaces with real walls.** Affinity groups choose their own membership, visibility, and guidelines — the platform enforces their boundaries without question and without visibility to outsiders or admins.
- **INCLUSION-4 · Nobody left behind in migration.** We move room by room, bridge to Discord as long as needed, and treat "I can't get in" as a P0 bug and a pastoral matter. The migration is finished when the last willing person is across, not when the Discord goes quiet.
- **INCLUSION-5 · Multilingual welcome.** The front door and safety-critical pages are translated into the languages our members actually speak (starting set to be discerned in Phase 0), and translation is a standing invitation, not a one-time task.
- **INCLUSION-6 · Consent before exposure.** Joining a room, appearing in "who's here," being named in a digest — all opt-in-shaped, never ambient.

**How we hold ourselves accountable:** Each season, ask who is missing from the Commons who used to be there, and who never arrived. Listen, then change something real.

## 5. Ethical Mindfulness

**Why:** "Ethical mindfulness" is our discipline of asking *should* before *can*. The Bible writers call anything that wounds right relationship "sin" — and we've all met software that wounds. We build slowly, honestly, and accountably, remembering the system encodes our values whether we intend it or not.

**Commitments:**

- **ETHICS-1 · Restraint is a feature.** No engagement optimization, no streak-guilt (our game streaks *celebrate*, they never scold), no "missing out" engineering, no dark patterns. See the explicit [won't-build list](04-product-principles.md#what-we-will-not-build).
- **ETHICS-2 · Abundance, not scarcity, in the bones.** No leaderboards of worth, no rankings of people, no reputation scores. Reputation is narrative, not mathematical (a ZAO Bank principle, now a Commons principle). Where we count anything, we count *participation and gifts*, never *people's standing*.
- **ETHICS-3 · Boring, honest, open tech.** Simple, well-documented, mainstream choices that any competent developer could inherit — because sustainability is an ethical duty to a community whose caretaker must be allowed to rest. The code lives in the open.
- **ETHICS-4 · Sustainability before features.** The project is paced for volunteer energy: rest is scheduled, bus-factor is documented, and "who can replace me?" is answered in writing for every role.
- **ETHICS-5 · Vendors are examined like visitors.** Any company we depend on is assessed (ownership, labor, surveillance, litigation history) and reassessed. Big-tech dependence is minimized, and what remains is documented honestly rather than pretended away.
- **ETHICS-6 · Every automation gets a conscience check.** Before any automated action (hiding flagged content, digests, reminders), we ask: what does this do *to* the most vulnerable person it touches, and can a human always overrule it?

**How we hold ourselves accountable:** The [Decision Log](09-decision-log.md) records every significant choice with its "why," tied to these commitments, and when to revisit it. We will disagree in the open and repent in the open.

---

## When these conflict

Priorities resolve toward **the protection and participation of the most vulnerable person affected** — and then toward honesty about what we gave up. Every such trade-off is written in the Decision Log, so the community can revisit it in the light.

*These non-negotiables can only be changed by community discernment — the same process that produced them.*
